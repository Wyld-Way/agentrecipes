> A recipe is a plug-and-play artifact someone else can implement fresh in their
> own stack — abstracted knowledge, never our code. Written for three readers at
> once: a maintainer, a DIY builder, and an open-source contributor.

## What a recipe IS

One proven system, abstracted to its container: the decisions, the steps, the
config shapes, the guardrails — with the content slots left open. The reader
implements it in their own codebase in hours-to-days, without ever seeing ours.
The clean-room test is the bar: if a team that is legally forbidden from copying
our code can implement the recipe, it's a recipe. If they'd need our source, it's
documentation, not a recipe.

## What makes ours different: receipts

Every recipe carries an evidence ledger — real production numbers, real failure
modes, real costs, each claim cited or marked ESTIMATE. A recipe without receipts
is a <MaturityBadge level="draft" /> and is labeled so. This is the
claims-inflation discipline turned into the product's moat: the internet is full
of untested prompt libraries; nobody ships the receipts.

## Anatomy (required sections, in order)

Every recipe opens with frontmatter:

```yaml
---
name: <human title>
slug: <kebab-case>
version: 0.1
maturity: draft | internal | field-tested | proven   # see Lifecycle below
origin: <the system this was abstracted from>
consumers: [<who has implemented it, incl. us>]
license: <CC-BY-SA-4.0 default when published; internal until then>
---
```

1. **What you get** — the outcome in 2–3 sentences, in the reader's terms.
2. **Use when / skip when** — honest fit filter, including who this is NOT for.
3. **Ingredients** — services, models, accounts, and expected costs. BYO
   everything; a recipe never requires our infrastructure.
4. **The recipe** — numbered steps. Each step names the decision being made and
   why, then the move. Config/schema shapes and pseudocode allowed; our source
   code never. `{{SLOTS}}` mark where the reader's content goes.
5. **The receipts** — the evidence ledger: real production numbers, cost ranges
   (quote the range, never the floor), what n the evidence is from. In the recipe
   body, numbers are attributed only to "origin production" — the detailed
   citations live in the internal evidence file (see Rules).
6. **What might go wrong** — failure modes abstracted from real incidents: the
   conditions under which each one bites, the symptom, and the move that prevents
   it. Every recipe includes at least one lesson learned the hard way — written
   so the reader recognizes their own risk, not our history.
7. **The pulse** — who or what keeps this alive after implementation: eval
   cadence, staleness checks, drift alarms. A system with no pulse dies quietly;
   a recipe that omits the pulse sells a stall.
8. **Provenance** — where the pattern came from, what it superseded, license.

## Rules

- **Container, not content.** Templatize the fixed shapes; leave the client's
  content as slots. Pre-filled content anchors wrong.
- **Receipts or the draft label.** No production evidence →
  <MaturityBadge level="draft" />, visibly. Contributors are held to the same
  bar.
- **Abstraction is the legal boundary.** Recipes carry patterns and shapes, never
  code lifted from a proprietary repo. This is what lets an AGPL or clean-room
  project consume a recipe safely.
- **The pulse section is mandatory.** Non-negotiable, learned the hard way.
- **Abstract the scar, keep the lesson.** The recipe body never carries
  origin-identifying details — no internal ticket ids, incident names, version
  numbers, file paths, or dates. Failure modes read as "what might go wrong when
  X" — the generalized condition, not a war story. The full citations backing
  every number and scar live in an internal evidence file beside the recipe,
  which never publishes: it's how we hold the claims-inflation bar without
  shipping our case files.
- **One recipe, one system.** Compose recipes; don't write omnibuses.
- **Public by default.** A recipe is publishable unless flagged closed. Openness
  IS the strategy — the library only compounds if recipes flow to it.

Maturity is the honesty signal, not a permission gate. A draft or internal recipe
may still publish — labeled as such — so long as the label tells the truth about
how much evidence stands behind it.
